# Places and the Recycling Lab

The City’s Places menu combines physical learning destinations with the existing workspace links. Selecting **Go to site** moves the Champion to a safe approach in Explore; the shared nearby prompt opens the activity within 12 metres. Workshop handoffs still open their requested activity directly after both activity hosts mount. Older `?batch=` links open the earlier recycling exercise.

`city-builder/places.js` owns the destination registry and bounded menu. Register a stable `id`, bilingual `labels: {en, zh}`, physical `available` status, an `arrival: {x, z}` when available, and an `enter` callback. An optional `target` controls the Champion’s facing on arrival. Future learning sites use the same registry and prompt without adding permanent HUD buttons. Unavailable physical sites use **Open practice venue**.

`city-common/recycling-site.js` resolves one sorter site before City scenery is generated. The existing mission Lab model’s main double doors and awning face **north (−Z)**; the tanks and rear service door face south. Its arrival point is three metres outside the north frontage. Centred forecourts are tried first, then lateral offsets on that frontage. The nearest safe Lab to spawn wins, with coordinate tie breaks. Buildings, roads, saved props, gateway lots and world bounds remain clear.

A city with no Lab may receive a deterministic runtime Lab and forecourt. This scenery never modifies the saved planner layout. A city whose existing Labs have blocked frontage receives no extra permanent Lab; the same composition opens as a temporary practice venue, with an explanation and Planner link. The generated model has a labelled procedural fallback if loading fails.

Reservation bounds protect the complete forecourt and any generated Lab from scenery and new prop placement. The seven-metre garbage truck docks along local X, with its cab pointing away from the hopper. The model’s named front wheels establish its authored +Z front; a −90° rotation and measured bounds place its rear 0.25 metres from the hopper. Its actual rotated bounds become a separate collision shape. A failed truck load leaves the docking bay empty. Gameplay collision uses the building and equipment shapes separately, keeping the entrance strip traversable. Travel rechecks live buildings, props, parked cars and traffic, searches nearby approaches if necessary, exits driving/flying, clears navigation and held input, grounds the Champion, and updates the camera in the same action. A blocked search leaves the Champion’s position unchanged.

Verification lives in `tests/recycling-site.test.mjs` and `tests/e2e/places.spec.mjs`, alongside the existing recycling, driving and Workshop round-trip tests. Local review captures are written to `/private/tmp/places-*.png` and `/private/tmp/recycling-lab-desktop.png`; they are not production assets.

## Connected sorter presentation

`city-common/recycling-layout.js` defines the 28 × 18 metre forecourt, equipment anchors, bin bank, queue slots and transport path. `recycling-site.js` transforms those same equipment footprints into world collision bounds, including site yaw. Bin footprints include their chutes and sloped label panels; the entrance strip remains clear. Camera framing uses the complete equipment bounds and the actual space beside or above the existing activity panel.

`city-builder/recycling-presentation.js` owns geometry, labels, truck loading, item wrappers, updates and disposal. Shared materials and instanced static parts keep the additional detail bounded. The off-white scanner portal, graphite conveyors, roller beds, motor and framed bins share one visual language. Material colours and destination order remain unchanged; human check uses amber. Both languages are printed on the bin faces.

The existing 30-step trial remains authoritative: an item reaches the scanner at phase 12, is classified by the existing trial, travels to the outlet and cross-belt, reaches its chosen gate, then drops into its collection slot. Gate, belt, indicator and object transforms depend only on trial state, so pause freezes the full machine and Step advances it consistently. No new saved schema or classification behaviour is introduced.

Each displayed row owns one persistent object. Eight waiting objects fit inside the hopper, with a clear pickup lane; the vacated queue position refills after the departing item clears the hopper. Remaining rows replenish this window. Presentation wrappers use measured bounds to centre, ground and scale the original meshes without changing the shared waste generators or training images. Collected stacks use measured heights; larger personal batches use smaller presentation wrappers to fit the collection bank.

`tests/e2e/recycling-presentation.spec.mjs` checks all seven destinations, deliberately wrong decisions, object identity, grounding, pause, reset, empty personal batches, queue replenishment, truck alignment, repeated opening and disposal. It captures desktop (1280), landscape tablet (1024), portrait tablet (834), opposite orbit and isolated detail views under Playwright’s output directory. The site unit tests also sample all transport paths and prove that pickup does not cross a waiting object.

Local rendering comparison (empty procedural equipment, identical camera and renderer, excluding the unchanged Lab, truck and waste): draw calls **67 → 43**, geometries **67 → 30**, materials **67 → 24**, textures **7 → 8**. The additional equipment raises triangles **734 → 5,392**. The resource regression rebuilds the facility six times and checks stable renderer counts; disposal releases all facility geometry and textures, while Three.js retains its internal transmission render target until renderer teardown. These are isolated equipment figures, not whole-City FPS claims.
